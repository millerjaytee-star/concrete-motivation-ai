# Concrete Motivation Client Acquisition System

## Goal
Create a respectful, repeatable system that turns researched relationships into discovery calls, pilot programs, paid engagements, referrals, and long-term partnerships.

## Ideal first clients
1. DC and Prince George's County schools and charter networks.
2. Youth workforce, mentoring, and violence-prevention organizations.
3. High school, college, club, and recreation athletic programs.
4. Churches and family-development ministries.
5. Hospitality, retail, and frontline employers needing leadership development.
6. Community conferences and local sponsors.

## Lead qualification
A lead is qualified when:
- the organization serves a priority audience;
- there is a visible program, event, challenge, or outcome relevant to the offer;
- a credible decision-maker or program contact is identifiable;
- Concrete Motivation has a specific reason to help;
- the proposed next step is proportionate and respectful.

## Research record
For every prospect, capture:
- organization and website;
- contact name, role, and verified email;
- mission and audience;
- current program or event;
- likely pressure/problem;
- relevant Concrete Motivation offer;
- personalization detail and source;
- last contact, next action, and status;
- consent/unsubscribe notes where applicable.

## Outreach sequence
### Day 1 — Relevant introduction
60–150 words. Lead with the organization's work, name the relevant outcome, explain the fit, and ask for a brief conversation. Do not attach a large deck on first contact unless requested.

### Day 4 — Useful follow-up
Add one useful idea: a sample session outline, suggested program format, or a question about priorities.

### Day 10 — Proof or pilot
Share a concise relevant resource or offer a low-risk pilot. Never invent proof.

### Day 21 — Close the loop
Respectfully close the sequence and leave the door open.

Stop after a clear no or unsubscribe request.

## Discovery call
1. Who is the audience?
2. What pressure or behavior is the organization trying to address?
3. What has already been tried?
4. What would a successful outcome look like?
5. What dates, format, accessibility, safety, and stakeholder requirements exist?
6. Who decides and what is the process?
7. What budget range or funding structure is available?
8. What evidence or reporting is expected?

## Proposal structure
- Situation and desired outcome.
- Recommended experience.
- Audience and delivery format.
- Learning objectives.
- Agenda and participant action.
- Deliverables and follow-up.
- Measurement method.
- Investment, payment schedule, and terms.
- Assumptions and client responsibilities.
- Next step and decision date.

## Initial offer menu
### Keynote
45–60 minutes plus optional Q&A. Best for awareness, shared language, and launch moments.

### Workshop
75–120 minutes. Includes exercises, commitments, and a practical tool.

### 30-Day Concrete Challenge
Kickoff session, weekly prompts, accountability, and completion review.

### Multi-session cohort
Four to eight sessions for youth, athletes, emerging leaders, or managers.

### Concrete Conversations Live
Moderated storytelling and practical dialogue with selected guests.

### Annual partner program
Quarterly experiences, digital support, reporting, and leadership alignment.

## Gmail rules
- Draft first; owner reviews and sends.
- No purchased bulk lists.
- Personalize from verifiable public information.
- Do not imply an existing relationship that does not exist.
- Use a clear sender identity and reply address.
- Keep records of sends, replies, and opt-outs.
- Never include confidential personal stories without approval.

## Weekly sales rhythm
### Monday
Research and qualify 10–15 organizations. Prepare first-contact drafts.

### Tuesday
Send approved introductions. Update CRM.

### Wednesday
Relationship day: calls, events, LinkedIn engagement, and partner conversations.

### Thursday
Follow-ups, discovery calls, and proposals.

### Friday
Review metrics, improve messages, request referrals from delivered work, and prepare next week.

## Dashboard
Track:
- new qualified leads;
- personalized contacts sent;
- replies and positive replies;
- discovery calls scheduled;
- proposals sent;
- bookings and contract value;
- source of each opportunity;
- time from first contact to booking;
- reasons won/lost;
- testimonials and referrals obtained with consent.

## First campaign
**Theme:** Leadership Before the Title

**Audience:** Schools, youth workforce programs, athletic organizations, and frontline employers.

**Promise:** Participants leave with a practical way to face pressure, own their next action, communicate responsibly, build one repeatable discipline, and identify an accountability relationship.

**Call to action:** A 20-minute fit conversation or a pilot session.
